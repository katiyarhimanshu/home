Learning Wireless Communication with USRP and GNU Radio

Software-Defined Radio (SDR) bridges the gap between textbook equations and real electromagnetic waves—allowing students and researchers to build live transceivers in software.

Moving Beyond Simulation to Over-the-Air Signals

While MATLAB and Python simulations are indispensable for analyzing modulation schemes and bit error rates (BER), real-world wireless channels introduce carrier frequency offset (CFO), phase noise, multipath fading, and hardware non-linearities. Combining Universal Software Radio Peripheral (USRP) hardware with GNU Radio gives learners direct, hands-on control over the physical layer.

Inside the SDR Workflow

  • USRP RF Front-End: Handles analog up/down-conversion, filtering, and high-speed ADC/DAC sampling, streaming raw baseband In-phase and Quadrature (I/Q) samples over Gigabit Ethernet or USB 3.0 to a host computer.
  • GNU Radio Companion (GRC): Provides a visual block-based environment where signal processing blocks—filters, FFTs, Costas loops, symbol synchronizers, and OFDM modulators—are wired into real-time C++/Python flowgraphs.

Recommended Lab Experiments for Students

  1. FM Radio Receiver & Spectrum Analyzer: Capture live broadcast FM signals, visualize baseband spectra, and demodulate audio in real time.
  2. Digital Modulation Transceiver (BPSK / QPSK / QAM): Transmit text or image packets between two USRP nodes and observe constellation diagrams under varying SNR and antenna distances.
  3. Timing & Carrier Synchronization: Experimentally observe how channel impairments rotate constellations and how phase-locked loops restore symbol lock.
Lab Tip: Always use attenuators when connecting USRP TX and RX ports directly via coaxial cables, and verify local ISM frequency regulations before attaching antennas for over-the-air transmission.
